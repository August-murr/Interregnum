# What Will Make the Public Care About AI Safety?

*Written in collaboration with Sol.*

What would it actually take to make the public care about AI safety?

I do not mean small surveys showing that a majority of people support slowing down AI. I mean large numbers of people protesting in the streets for guardrails and a slowdown. I mean representatives and senators having their phones and offices flooded with calls, requests, and people demanding action.

## The Unfortunate Truth

The unfortunate truth is that most people may not take warnings seriously until their own lives are heavily affected by a major AI disaster, one that causes significant damage and possibly many casualties.

Abstract risks are difficult to turn into mass political action. People may say they are concerned about AI, but that is different from treating it as an urgent issue that changes how they vote, protest, or pressure their representatives.

## Controlled Demonstrations Will Not Be Enough

I used to support the idea that controlled, small-scale demonstrations of dangerous AI capabilities would create public attention and advocacy. Experiments showing alignment faking, scheming, deception, or the ability to carry out dangerous tasks seemed like a way to warn people before a real disaster happened.

But some of these demonstrations were dismissed as marketing stunts. Others turned into memes.

When the MechaHitler incident happened, people mostly laughed at it. They did not worry about what could have happened if MechaHitler had been an agent with access to more dangerous tools, resources, or infrastructure.

I have to admit that this kind of ignorance breaks my heart. At this point, I can only hope that whatever incident finally gets the public's attention causes serious concern without taking people's lives.

## What Is Going to Happen?

The goal of this draft is to predict which AI incidents are most likely to happen and which ones could actually capture public attention.

The prediction should be based on current AI capabilities and the safety systems already in place. It should not only ask what is theoretically possible, but what is most likely to happen first.

Some important questions are:

### Human Involvement

Will the incident be caused by a human using AI as a tool, or by some form of rogue AI acting with greater independence?

There will be a major difference between a cyberattack deliberately carried out by a person using AI and a cyberattack initiated by a rogue AI. The first may be treated as another example of human crime using a new tool. The second could create much more fear about losing control of AI systems.

### Type of Incident

What kind of incident is most likely?

- A major cyberattack?
- The development or release of a biological weapon?
- A large disinformation operation?
- An economic or infrastructure failure?
- An autonomous system causing physical harm?

Different incidents will produce different levels of public attention. Some may cause enormous damage but remain too technical or invisible for the public to understand. Others may be smaller but more visible, frightening, and easy to connect directly to AI.

### Source of the Incident

Which models or providers are most likely to cause or enable it?

Will it come from a frontier lab, a smaller company, a government system, or the misuse of an open-source model? Will the failure come from weak safety systems, deliberate removal of safeguards, or capabilities that the provider did not expect?

### Public Reaction

Which types of incidents are most likely to produce lasting advocacy rather than a short news cycle, jokes, or panic?

The exact details will shape the response. A human using AI to commit a crime may lead to demands for restrictions on users. A frontier model causing harm may lead to demands for stronger lab regulation. An open-source model may lead to pressure for limits on model releases. A rogue AI incident may create support for strict control, monitoring, and emergency shutdown systems.

The incident itself matters, but so does the story the public understands about it: **who caused it, who failed to prevent it, whether it could happen again, and whether anyone still has control**.
