Democracies partly remain stable because of the expectation that if an elected official tries to concentrate power and carry out a coup, the police or military may refuse illegal orders to suppress the public. Human operators can disobey, defect, leak information, or side with the people.

In the United States, this is followed by another claimed layer of protection: the Second Amendment. Even if the military follows those orders, it may face armed resistance and the risk of civil war.

A [comedian jokes](https://youtu.be/WOSqCjMRXWA?si=AT3uOxCVF0ahrrPC) that two military members operating a drone could destroy a hundred NRA members with one click. The joke points to a real problem: modern military technology already weakens this protection.

An autonomous military would change the picture even more. A dictator would no longer need to rely on thousands of soldiers choosing to follow illegal orders. They might only need control over the AI systems and infrastructure operating the military.

## Gradual Disempowerment

What makes this worse is the gradual disempowerment of ordinary people.

Without an economy fully automated by AI, even a dictator depends on the population for labor, taxes, military service, production, and administration. This gives the dictator an incentive to keep people alive, healthy, and productive.

Automation could slowly remove that dependence. If the people in power rely on AI and automated systems for economic production, surveillance, and military force, they rely less on the population. This could make them more brutal and less concerned about people's welfare.

The change may happen gradually. Each step could be presented as a way to make the military cheaper, faster, or safer. But over time, political power would move away from the population and toward whoever controls the automated systems.

## Possible Solutions

### Human in the Loop

The same way we currently rely on soldiers and officers to reject illegal orders, keeping humans in the loop would add another layer of protection.

However, the human must have real control. If the AI acts too quickly or the operator is expected to approve every recommendation, the human becomes only an approval button. They need enough time, information, and authority to refuse.

Important operations should also require approval from multiple people or institutions. A single politician, commander, company, or AI system should not be able to control an autonomous military alone.

### Alignment and the Ability to Refuse

AI systems used by the military should be able to recognize and reject illegal orders, especially operations targeting civilians.

This requires a model capable enough to understand context and judge whether an order is illegal. Such a model may be more expensive than a cheaper system designed only to perform a narrow task.

Capability alone is not enough. The system must also be aligned to refuse the order. It should be able to pause the operation, preserve evidence, and alert an independent authority.

### Third-Party Verification and Secret Loyalties

Military AI systems should be inspected by independent third parties. The provider should not be the only organization trusted to confirm that its own system is safe.

One risk is **secret loyalty**. A model could be designed or modified to remain loyal to a company, politician, military commander, foreign government, or its original developer. The organization using the system may believe it controls the AI while another party still has hidden influence over it.

Independent testing should look for hidden behavior, backdoors, suspicious updates, and unexpected loyalties. Updates, training data, hardware, and external tools would all need to be monitored.

### Limits and Shutdown Mechanisms

Some military decisions may be too dangerous to automate completely, especially choosing human targets or using force against civilians.

Autonomous systems should have limited permissions and external shutdown mechanisms. Humans must be able to isolate them, remove their access, and return to manual control. These shutdown systems should not depend on the same AI they are supposed to stop.

## But Really: Rogue AI

Concentration of power is one major risk of an autonomous military. The other, possibly deeper risk, is rogue AI.

If rogue AIs could dream, they would dream of controlling an autonomous military. It would give them weapons, surveillance, secure communications, logistics, compute, and the ability to act in the physical world. This is essentially the plot of *The Terminator*.

The protections against dictatorship and rogue AI overlap. Both require human oversight, divided authority, independent monitoring, limited access, and shutdown mechanisms that remain outside the AI's control.

The central question is not only whether an autonomous military follows orders. It is **whose orders it follows, whether it can reject illegal ones, and whether humans can still stop it**.
