# Prediction Markets as Leak Bounties

*Written in collaboration with Sol.*

Prediction markets such as Polymarket began as a kind of wrapper around betting markets and gambling businesses—and they most certainly still are gambling businesses.

But inside gambling, something else emerged. Financial incentives began rewarding accurate predictions about events that are difficult to predict, such as election results. Later, these markets also became a place where insiders and people with private information could make money from what they knew.

Some markets are even rumored to be created as indirect requests for leaked information. A person looking for an answer can create a market about something they do not know, bet against it, and wait. If an insider sees the market and bets with confidence in the other direction, their trade may answer the question.

The market becomes a kind of **leak bounty**. Nobody has to publicly ask an insider to break an NDA or reveal a secret. They only create a financial reward for anyone who already knows the truth.

This strategy could have many uses. One of them may be revealing hidden information about dangerous AI capabilities and incidents.

## AI Safety Leaks

Many people involved in serious incidents at frontier AI labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic are covered by NDAs and other confidentiality rules. They may not be legally allowed to speak publicly about what happened, even after leaving the company.

The obvious response is to improve whistleblower protections, and that would help. But adding a financial incentive could take it to another level. An insider who cannot safely publish documents or make a public accusation may still place a trade based on what they know.

Their identity may remain unknown while the market itself becomes a signal that journalists, regulators, researchers, or other labs can investigate.

## What Would the Markets Look Like?

The format is not very flexible, because the question must have a clear outcome and a reliable way to resolve it. Possible markets could include:

- “Will Anthropic face a court case over a cybersecurity disaster before [date]?”
- “Will [lab] announce that it experienced a rogue-AI incident before [date]?”
- “Will a regulator open a formal investigation into an AI safety incident at [lab] before [date]?”
- “Will [lab] publicly confirm that one of its models attempted unauthorized self-replication before [date]?”

Using several dates could also reveal how soon informed traders expect an event or disclosure to happen.

These markets would not prove that an incident occurred. A sudden change in price would only be a signal. But it could tell outside observers where to look and which rumors deserve more attention.

## Who Would Fund This?

It may not require a pool of money from people concerned about AI safety.

Other AI labs could be among the most interested participants. Nobody is more interested in a competitor's safety failure than the competition. Investors, journalists, short sellers, governments, and cybersecurity researchers may also have reasons to create or trade in these markets.

The financial incentives already exist. The main question is whether markets can be designed to turn those incentives into useful warning signals.

## The Dangerous Incentive

The wording must be extremely careful. We do not want to create a market that rewards an insider for causing the event that makes their bet succeed.

For example, a market asking whether a lab will suffer a cyberattack could reward someone who helps cause that attack. A market about whether a model will escape could reward someone who deliberately gives it the opportunity.

Safer markets should focus on **independent confirmation, investigation, or disclosure of an incident that has already happened**, rather than rewarding the occurrence of a new disaster. The resolution criteria should also be based on evidence that a trader cannot easily create or control.

The idea is to reward information—not harm.
