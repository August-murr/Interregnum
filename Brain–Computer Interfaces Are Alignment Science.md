# Brain–Computer Interfaces Are Alignment Science

*Written in collaboration with Sol.*

Brain–computer interfaces are not usually included in conversations or research discussions about AI alignment. However, they belong within alignment science—and perhaps even superalignment.

Many alignment methods benefit from increasing compute, and machine learning has already been used in brain–computer interfaces for years. When we discuss alignment, we often focus on safety, adversarial robustness, red teaming, interpretability, and governance. Brain–computer interfaces rarely receive the same attention.

Yet they may be essential to possibilities such as human–AI symbiosis. More broadly, they could contribute to technical solutions for how humans and AI, as a newly emerging kind of intelligence or species, might coexist and benefit one another.

This direction may be neglected partly because progress in brain–computer interfaces has been slower and more difficult than progress in many other areas. It may also feel less urgent because it proposes a long-term path for coexistence rather than an immediate response to short-term safety problems.

The broader ideas of AI symbiosis and species-level coexistence also feel almost alien to us. We do not yet have clear ways to perceive, frame, or approach them. That uncertainty may itself be one reason the topic deserves a place within alignment research.
