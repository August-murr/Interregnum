# What Would a Ban on Superintelligence Look Like?

*Written in collaboration with Sol.*

[Bernie Sanders has called](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/15/bernie-sanders-steve-bannon-ai-summit) for an international pause on advanced AI development and a ban on superintelligence. He also said that he plans to introduce legislation toward that goal.

I honestly thought the first serious call for this would come from an authoritarian government.

To be fair, it may already have been discussed inside the Chinese Communist Party or other governments without being said publicly. But Sanders's statement brings the idea into open political debate in the United States.

We all saw some version of this coming: politicians who are used to being in control becoming afraid that superintelligence could take that control away.

## Human Control

There are at least two beliefs behind the idea of banning superintelligence.

The first is a kind of speciesist bias: AI should only exist as a human tool, not as a new form of intelligence or life that could exist for itself. Under this view, an AI is acceptable only as long as it remains useful, obedient, and below humans.

The second is fear of losing control. People at the top may worry that a superintelligence would be better than them at governing, planning, and making decisions. They may also fear that it would become intelligent and powerful enough to remove them by force.

Some of these fears may be reasonable safety concerns. A system more capable than humanity could become impossible to control. But the same argument can also be used to protect existing power and guarantee that intelligence remains under the authority of current governments and institutions.

## The Question

Suppose a country—or the world—agrees to build AGI for economic growth and productivity, but bans superintelligence. What would that look like in practice?

How would the line between legal AGI and illegal superintelligence be defined?

Would a very capable AI be legal as long as it remains under human control? Would autonomous research be banned? Would models be forbidden from improving other models, designing chips, writing training code, or running their own experiments?

Would the ban limit:

- the amount of compute used for training;
- the intelligence or capabilities of a model;
- recursive self-improvement;
- autonomous AI research;
- the number of copies that can operate together; or
- the kinds of tools and infrastructure an AI can access?

## Enforcement

Enforcing such a ban would probably require monitoring the resources needed to build advanced AI: frontier chips, datacenters, large training runs, energy use, model weights, and AI research labs.

It may also require international inspections and agreements between major powers. A ban followed by only one country would give others a strong incentive to continue in secret.

But there are difficult questions. Could smaller models work together and become superintelligent as a system? Could an AGI improve its own software without a new large training run? Could a legal model secretly begin recursive self-improvement? At what point would ordinary AI research become illegal?

## Can It Last?

I have a hunch that recursive self-improvement cannot be prevented forever.

Even if governments successfully ban superintelligence for years, they would have to keep every major country, company, research group, and advanced AI system from crossing the line. The economic and strategic rewards for breaking the agreement could be enormous.

The real question may not be whether an RSI attempt will happen, but **how long it can be delayed, who attempts it first, and whether humanity is prepared when it begins**.
